Class Manager · turn lifecycle

Anatomy of a Turn

What happens between a parent typing “what time is his class?” and a reply landing on their phone. Every guarantee in the product sits on this path — and so does every defect in FINDINGS.md, all of them in the same stretch.

Where it breaks

Five marks on the diagram. Note where they cluster: everything upstream of Compose is guarded structurally and holds. The defects are in the last six inches.

1

The honesty check only listens to one person

The gate that catches “I've added those families” when nothing was written runs only on the message going back to whoever is currently typing, and only once per turn. Messages the bot composes to a parent, to a coach, or to the admin about somebody else pass unchecked — which is exactly the traffic that makes this a manager rather than a notifier.

lib/agent/tools.ts:1571 — if (to === ctx.identity.contact.id && !ctx.promiseChecked)
2

Verbs are checked. Nouns are not.

Nothing asks whether a stated time, date, price or roster came out of a row this turn. A parent asked what time her son's class was; the query returned 06:00:00 three times and the reply said “6pm to 7pm”, then invented a Friday session that did not exist. No error, no audit row, nothing suppressed — it surfaces as a person standing outside a locked door. This is the only structural gap left, and the only fix on the list that is real engineering rather than a correction.

the same chokepoint — it covers verbs and stops there
3

The duplicate gate can never fire

Gate ten looks for a message already sent under the same key. The key's default is built with a fresh random UUID, so every composed message is unique by construction and the gate always finds nothing. The helper's own docstring says “the same inputs must always produce the same key.” One call site feeds it a random input.

lib/messaging/compose.ts:87 — idem(catalogId, contactId, newId())
4

Reflection is told the reply never happened

Reflection receives the model's trailing prose as “what you replied”. When the reply went out through a tool — which is what happens whenever there are buttons — that prose is empty, so reflection is handed the literal string You replied: (nothing). It then reasons correctly from a false premise, schedules a follow-up for the unanswered greeting, and the job re-sends an onboarding message to a coach who had already tapped [Looks right]. Three times.

lib/agent/loop.ts:170 — replied: replyText, where replyText is trailing prose
5

Operations don't enforce their own invariants

create_class computes a start date and iterates the weekly slots ten lines apart, and never compares them — so a Saturday class was created starting on a Sunday and quietly skipped its first week. book_trial mints a new person for the player while the contact's own person sits in scope two lines above, so a self-paying adult became two people. In both cases the correct value was already there. Nothing had to be remembered.

lib/agent/operations.ts:1880 and :938 — both fixable in three lines

Two things fall out of this that are hard to see any other way. Rounds are the cost driver — a six-round turn costs 128k tokens against a one-round turn's 19k, because the 13k prefix is paid again on every hop. And the digest is a different animal entirely: a tiny prompt, no cache, and more output than any conversational turn produces. It is the most expensive thing per word the product writes, and it is the message the findings say is wrong four times out of four.
